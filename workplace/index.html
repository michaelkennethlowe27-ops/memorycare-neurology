The Evidence

The window opens earlier than people assume.

Four findings from the peer-reviewed literature. Every source is linked below.

  • 1
    Measurable change begins in your forties, not your sixties. A ten-year study of 7,390 office workers found detectable decline in reasoning and memory in the group aged 45 to 49 at baseline. Singh-Manoux et al., BMJ, 2012
  • 2
    Much of it is modifiable. The 2024 Lancet Commission identified fourteen risk factors that, addressed across a lifetime, are associated with an estimated 45% of dementia cases worldwide. Livingston et al., The Lancet, 2024
  • 3
    Sleep is not a character issue. Across 70 studies and 147 cognitive tests, short-term sleep loss produced its largest effect on sustained attention — the capacity that holds a long meeting or a complex document together. Lim & Dinges, Psychological Bulletin, 2010
  • 4
    Cognition can be measured objectively. The assessment used here was validated against a normative sample of 1,069 people aged 7 to 90, with reliability comparable to conventional pencil-and-paper neuropsychological testing. Gualtieri & Johnson, Archives of Clinical Neuropsychology, 2006

How It Works

Two touches. One plan.

01

Measure

A validated computerized assessment that takes about 45 minutes. Most online cognitive screens run ten and measure almost nothing. You take this one at your desk or at home, on a computer if you have one, or on a tablet or phone. It scores nine cognitive domains against age-matched norms. Dr. Lowe reviews your results before you speak.

02

Interpret

An unhurried consultation — typically 45 to 60 minutes, with no hard stop. Where you stand against your peers, what is driving it, and which levers are actually worth pulling.

03

Act

Within a week: a written plan, and the peer-reviewed research behind every recommendation in it.
