Why You Are Here

The lists you make and lose. The deadline that only becomes real at midnight. The book you have started four times.

You may have suspected this about yourself for a very long time. Or you may have only just started wondering — sitting in a doctor's office while someone described your child, recognizing far more of it in yourself than you expected.

Either way, the usual next step is a questionnaire. You answer twenty items about yourself from memory, and a number comes out. That is genuinely useful information. It is also entirely self-reported, and it cannot distinguish lifelong attention difficulty from the four or five other things that produce exactly the same complaint.

The Evidence

What is actually established.

Four findings from the peer-reviewed literature. Every source is linked below.

  • 1
    Adults are the larger and less-examined group. ADHD occurs in roughly 5.9% of young people and 2.5% of adults — a population many times the number who were ever evaluated as children. Faraone et al., Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 2021
  • 2
    It runs in families, strongly. Across 37 twin studies, the average heritability is 74% — higher than most conditions in psychiatry. Parents frequently recognize their own history in a child's evaluation. Faraone & Larsson, Molecular Psychiatry, 2019
  • 3
    Attention complaints are not specific. Across 70 studies and 147 cognitive tests, ordinary sleep loss produced its largest effect on exactly the capacity people describe when they say they cannot focus. Lim & Dinges, Psychological Bulletin, 2010
  • 4
    Attention can be measured rather than described. The assessment used here was validated against a normative sample of 1,069 people aged 7 to 90, with reliability comparable to conventional pencil-and-paper neuropsychological testing. Gualtieri & Johnson, Archives of Clinical Neuropsychology, 2006

How It Works

Two touches. One plan.

01

Measure

A validated computerized assessment that takes about 45 minutes. Most online cognitive screens run ten and measure almost nothing. You take this one at home, on a computer if you have one, or on a tablet or phone. It scores nine cognitive domains against age-matched norms, including sustained attention and cognitive flexibility.

02

Interpret

An unhurried consultation — typically 45 to 60 minutes, with no hard stop. What the numbers show, and a careful look at sleep, mood, thyroid, medication, and the other common drivers of the same symptoms.

03

Act

Within a week: a written plan, and the peer-reviewed research behind every recommendation in it.

This is not a diagnosis, and not a prescription.

The World Federation of ADHD is unambiguous on this point: ADHD is diagnosed through clinical interview and history, and cannot be diagnosed by rating scales, neuropsychological testing, or brain imaging. No computerized test can hand you that answer, whatever the telehealth stimulant mills imply.

The Brain Health Blueprint does not diagnose ADHD and does not prescribe or manage stimulant medication. What it gives you is the objective layer beneath the questionnaire — real numbers on the domains attention actually lives in, benchmarked against your age, plus a structured review of everything else that produces the same picture.
